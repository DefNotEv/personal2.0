For me, collecting isn’t just a hobby; it’s a way of preserving the moments and memories that shape who I am. Whether it's pins, trading cards, lanyards, or a 600GB archive of digital photos, my collections represent more than just things—they're windows into my experiences. Let me walk you through my world of memories, one collection at a time.

## Pins

At nine, I was first introduced to Disney pin trading by my best friend during a cruise. What began as a small souvenir hunt soon transformed into an obsession. After all, why stop at Disney? I began collecting pins from every new place I visited. Today, my leather-bound pin book holds sixty-two pins from thirty-two Disney collections and twenty-three different countries. Each pin reminds me of a specific moment, a place, or a story worth sharing. Bringing this book to show-and-tell year after year not only kept the memories alive, but also allowed me to share my adventures with others. In a sense, my pin collection became a visual diary of the places I’ve explored.

## Trading Cards

A year after my pin collection began, my dad introduced me to *Magic: The Gathering*. When he was younger, he played competitively, and it was his dream to share the game with his kids. While my mom wasn’t thrilled with the ever-growing (and expensive) stacks of cards, Magic became more than just a collection—it became our family’s way of bonding. Whether we’re stuck in a hotel room or looking for something to do on a rainy day, we can always say, “Let’s play Magic.” Now, I own over ten decks and more than a thousand cards. Each match isn’t just a game—it’s another memory made with my family.

## Lanyards

My lanyard collection started by accident after attending a summer program at the University of British Columbia in my sophomore year. Everyone at the program wore a lanyard that we all got to keep. A few days later, I picked up a UBC lanyard as a souvenir, and soon, my collection grew. Over the years, I've accumulated lanyards from hackathons, universities, and conferences, including one from North America’s biggest tech conference. Each one represents an event where I learned something new or met someone inspiring. My lanyards show the cool extracurriculars I’ve done and my dedication to making an impact in STEM. My favorite lanyard though? A DoorDash lanyard gifted by my uncle, a reminder of the people who’ve supported me along the way.

## Polaroids

In junior year, my ex-boyfriend offered me a spare Polaroid camera. Despite the steep cost of film, I instantly fell in love with the art of capturing moments in their rawest, uneditable form. Polaroids are different from digital photos—each one is a one-of-a-kind snapshot that can’t be altered or replicated.

## Digital Photos

While I love Polaroids, they’re not always practical for someone on a budget. Instead, I’ve become the unofficial photographer among my friends. Armed with my phone, I’m constantly capturing the little moments that matter most. I’ve been teased for being the one to “document everything,” but I embrace it. My camera roll, now over 600GB, is an archive of memories: from school events to late-night hangouts. Knowing I can revisit those memories whenever I want gives me a sense of comfort, especially when life feels chaotic.
  
  
  
  
So, what do I bring to show and tell? I bring more than just pins, cards, lanyards, or photographs. I bring the people I've met, the places I've been, and the experiences that have made me who I am. Each collection tells a different part of my story, and together, they paint a picture of someone who values connection, curiosity, and making memories. As I continue to grow, so will my collections, because I know there are still countless people to meet, places to explore, and stories worth remembering. And if there's one thing I've learned, it's that I'm not really collecting things. I'm collecting a life worth looking back on.
