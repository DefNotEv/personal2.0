---
title: Pink is the most significant colour.
date: 2026-09
version: motorsports
---

The other day, I was speaking to a younger white friend when he looked me dead in the eye and said "Minorities don't actually have any real problems. I mean, when I was in Japan, I was the minority and they LOVED me! Evelyn do you really think being an Asian woman gives you real problems?"

Real problems? I guess not. It’s hard to answer a question like that on the spot, because the honest answer isn’t a yes or no. It’s a color.

I was eleven years old at my first robotics meeting. I walked in with my pink iPad, the one I’d picked out myself and was a little proud of, and the guys in the room took one look at it and started laughing. A pink iPad meant I was there by accident. Maybe someone’s little sister. Definitely not someone who could build anything.

The funny part is that I was the only person in that entire room who could code.

That didn’t matter, though. Nobody asked what I could do. They’d already decided, and the pink was all the evidence they needed. I remember sliding the iPad back into my bag and thinking, okay, so that’s how this works. I changed my case to black that weekend.

The summer before I started university, I volunteered at my first motorsports race. I’d been looking forward to it for weeks. I was going to start studying motorsports engineering, and I was finally standing in the world I wanted to be part of.

The other volunteers spent the next hour grilling me. What’s the difference between oversteer and understeer? What does a diffuser do? Which series runs which engine? It wasn’t small talk. It was a test, and I could tell from their faces that they expected me to fail it. Nobody else got quizzed. Nobody else had to prove they belonged before they were allowed to just be there.

I barely passed. But it’s exhausting to pass a test that no one else has to take.

So here’s the thing I didn’t say to my friend: I’m scared of wearing pink.

Not because I don’t like it. I learned that pink is a signal. It tells people you’re soft, decorative, not serious. And I want to break into motorsport, an industry where I already walk in with two strikes against me before I open my mouth. I can’t control being a woman. I can’t control being Asian. But I can control the color of my clothes, so I leave the pink at home and show up in black in hopes it buys me a few extra seconds of being taken seriously.

I'm glad my friend was loved in Japan, but being welcomed as a novelty for a few weeks is not the same as having to earn your place every single time you walk into a room you’ve worked your whole life to stand in.

The problem was pink, and one day, I want to walk into a garage wearing it, and have the only thing anyone notices be that I know what I’m doing.
